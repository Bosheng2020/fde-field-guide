# FDE Skills Matrix

**Language:** English · [简体中文](../zh-CN/04-skills-matrix.md)  
**Content type:** Author synthesis and self-assessment rubric  
**Last reviewed:** 2026-10-22

Strong FDEs are T-shaped: sufficiently broad to own the whole path, with depth in one or more areas that matter to the product and customer.

## Proficiency scale

- **L1 — Understand:** explains concepts and contributes with guidance.
- **L2 — Deliver:** independently completes a bounded production task.
- **L3 — Lead:** designs and owns an ambiguous deployment across teams.
- **L4 — Multiply:** creates systems, standards, and coaching that raise the whole organization.

## Competency matrix

| Domain | L1 evidence | L2 evidence | L3 evidence | L4 evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Discovery and scoping | Maps a workflow | Defines a thin slice and metric | Renegotiates ambiguity into an outcome contract | Creates repeatable qualification/scoping system |
| End-to-end engineering | Contributes to one layer | Ships a complete small service/workflow | Owns multi-system production architecture | Establishes reusable platform primitives |
| AI / LLM systems | Uses models and tools correctly | Builds grounded/tool-using workflow | Diagnoses system vs model failure; manages trade-offs | Shapes model/product roadmap from field evidence |
| Evaluation | Writes test cases | Builds task evals and traces | Connects regressions to risk and business outcomes | Creates organization-wide evaluation practice |
| Reliability and security | Follows controls | Implements permissions, monitoring, fallbacks | Leads risk-based production readiness | Defines scalable governance and incident patterns |
| Product judgment | Understands user need | Prioritizes a valuable narrow workflow | Balances customer specificity and product leverage | Identifies category-level patterns across accounts |
| Customer communication | Explains technical work | Runs working sessions and difficult updates | Aligns executives, users, engineers, and risk teams | Develops trusted delivery culture and leaders |
| Adoption and value | Tracks usage | Designs rollout and measures outcome | Redesigns workflow/incentives to sustain adoption | Builds repeatable expansion and value system |
| Program execution | Manages own tasks | Sequences a bounded project | Leads multiple dependencies under pressure | Improves portfolio staffing and delivery system |
| Field-to-product learning | Reports anecdotes | Submits reproducible issues | Produces evals, components, and product proposals | Builds a compounding learning loop |

## Technical breadth checklist

An AI-focused FDE should be able to reason about:

- Python and/or TypeScript; APIs; schemas; databases; queues;
- frontend workflow and user interaction;
- data ingestion, retrieval, indexing, and lineage;
- model selection, prompting, context, structured output, tool calling, agents;
- evaluation sets, graders, traces, experiments, and regression testing;
- cloud, containers, CI/CD, secrets, networking, monitoring, and cost;
- authentication, authorization, tenancy, privacy, audit, and retention;
- human approval, escalation, rollback, and incident response.

This is not a keyword checklist. Evidence means building, debugging, or operating a complete path.

## Non-technical skills that are technical multipliers

- Listening for the underlying decision rather than the requested feature.
- Writing clear problem statements, trade-offs, and decision records.
- Delivering bad news early without losing trust.
- Working across cultures and levels of technical depth.
- Staying calm during incidents and executive pressure.
- Distinguishing urgency from importance.
- Knowing when to stop, simplify, or use deterministic software.

## Portfolio evidence

A credible portfolio project should show:

1. a real or carefully simulated workflow;
2. a baseline and explicit success criteria;
3. end-to-end architecture and operating constraints;
4. representative evaluations, not hand-picked prompts;
5. security and human-control decisions;
6. production deployment and telemetry;
7. failure analysis and iteration;
8. a measured result or honest explanation of why the project stopped.

The repository itself is less important than the evidence and decisions it makes inspectable.

## Self-assessment

For every domain, write one artifact or example proving your claimed level. If you cannot name evidence, rate yourself one level lower and design a project that produces it.
